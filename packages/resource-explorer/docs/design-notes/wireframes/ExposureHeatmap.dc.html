The exposure heatmap, and the three ways it can under-report

I said in round 1 that I would not draw this until the role side existed. It does: #193 shipped postgres_operations, whose privilege_audit reads pg_roles, information_schema.role_table_grants and pg_default_acl (connection.py:486), and stores grants in database_grants (registry.py:654). So it is drawable now — and drawing it against those two shapes is what produced the rest of this page.

The rule this whole artboard turns on. For every other representation in the brief, a missing measurement shows as a gap and the reader is mildly worse off. For an exposure view it is not symmetric: a cell that should say this role can read this column and instead says nothing is read as a clean bill of health. So an empty cell here must mean “no path found”, never “no path” — and every path the survey does not look down has to be named on the surface, not in a doc.

Two · the cells are table grants wearing column labels

get_privilege_audit() reads information_schema.role_table_grants, which is table-level. And database_grants cannot hold anything finer: its columns are schema_name, object_name, object_type, grantee, privilege_type, and its UNIQUE has no column dimension either.

One direction of that inheritance is sound: a role with SELECT on a table really can read every column in it, so a filled cell is true. The other direction is not. Postgres also supports column-level grants — GRANT SELECT (email) ON patient TO analyst_ro — and a column-only grant does not appear in role_table_grants at all. So a role given access to exactly the sensitive column, and nothing else, renders as a row of dashes.

The ask, and it is a migration, which is why it belongs in round 2: read information_schema.column_privileges instead. It returns column-level grants and table-level grants already expanded per column, so it is a strict superset of what is read today — one query, not two — and add column_name to database_grants and its UNIQUE. Then the grid's cells are measured at the grain they are drawn at, and the inheritance disappears rather than needing a caption.

column_privileges appears nowhere in the repo today. If the read stays table-level for now, the grid keeps panel one's footer sentence and the columns are labelled tables, not columns — capability may diverge, honesty may not.

Three · the third path: a role that inherits

privilege_audit reads pg_roles — the roles — but nothing reads role membership: pg_auth_members, pg_has_role and rolinherit are absent from the repo. Granting through a group role is the normal way to administer Postgres, so this is not an edge case: give SELECT to readers, put analyst_ro in readers, and the grid shows analyst_ro with no access to anything.

Two ways out, and the choice is the implementer's: read pg_auth_members and resolve the closure ourselves, or ask Postgres directly with has_column_privilege(role, table, column, 'SELECT'), which already accounts for membership, inheritance and PUBLIC in one call. The second is the honest answer to the question the grid actually asks — can this role read this column — rather than a reconstruction of it from three catalogs.

Whichever is chosen, a filled cell then needs to say which path it came by, because granted directly and inherited via readers are revoked in different places, and a reader who cannot tell will revoke the wrong one. The cell's detail popover carries it; the grid stays a grid.

Four · and the count opens what it counted

House rule, applied: the grid is not the answer, it is the question made visible. So the summary above it is a sentence with the paths in it, and each figure opens the rows behind it —

6 columns carry a sensitive class. 4 are readable by every role, through a PUBLIC grant on one table. 1 is readable by a role that need not read it. 1 was not measured.

— which is also why legacy.ssn_old is a row rather than an omission. SECTION_GRANTS (registry.py:479) already stores this survey never looked at grants as a fact, so the not-measured row is a read, not a new mechanism. It is the one part of this page that needs no build at all.
